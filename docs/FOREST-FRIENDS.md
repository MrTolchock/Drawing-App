# Waldpost implementation

The active game follows [letter sessions](LETTER-SESSIONS.md), [visual style](../design/VISUAL-STYLE.md) and the [puzzle blueprint](../design/PUZZLE-BLUEPRINT.md). Historical Heart Tree screens and engines live in `legacy/legacy-game/`; `?screen=forest` opens the current den. The old gallery URL opens the adult gate.

The catalogue lists all 31 [puzzles and competence assignments](PUZZLE-CATALOGUE.md). An interrupted puzzle resumes before the host rotates. Correct answers advance automatically; only completing all five consumes a letter. Non-invited friends, direct routes and the mailman cannot grant extra puzzles; a non-invited friend shows a small trick and points the way to the friend on the letter (`TRICKS` in `characters3d.js`, `visit` in `forest3d.js`).

The den table and picture pile remain scenery. Drawing/saved-picture controls and routes are retired; historical template IDs, localStorage and IndexedDB records remain intact. Archived story records are retained but their former screens are no longer shipped or downloaded. The new journey does not spend historical currencies.

The [local gameplay log](PLAY-LOG.md) records child iterations, selected answers, input issues, foreground times and journey observations. Settings exports JSON with exact task definitions and analysis summaries; no gameplay data is uploaded. It survives refresh/offline use in the same browser and can be cleared independently of the automatic journey state. Adult review previews are excluded from child outcomes.

Review decisions are shared in GitHub’s `puzzle-review-data` branch, with a separate local cache and offline outbox. Do not ship approvals, reset storage or silently carry an old approval to a changed revision. Only confirmed exact-revision GitHub approvals enter child play. Export/import is revision checked. See [review storage](REVIEW-STORAGE.md). Tests use isolated fixtures; the owner still inspects every real iteration before children use it.

The world supports requested rule speech, quiet puzzle-button/correct/retry sounds and parent sound preferences. Menus are silent. There is no countdown or automatic answer narration. Updates preserve saved data and defer reload during active puzzles and dialogs. See [Development](DEVELOPMENT.md) for validation and publishing.

The friends who live on the ground (Eichhörnchen, Biber, Igel, Dachs, and the Reh a little further) stroll a few slow steps round their home spot with pauses, keep off trails and crossings, stand and turn to Fuchs when he comes close (a brief greeting hop, then they look at him), and stay put with reduced motion. The squirrel bounds in short dashes with both hind legs together, glances round, flicks its tail and nibbles; the deer walks smoothly, grazes now and then with flicking ears and lifts its head when Fuchs comes (`MANNERS` in `characters3d.js`); their map pin follows them. Trees between the camera and Fuchs turn translucent in a narrow, soft-edged column (`js/friends/world/textures.js`).

The camera stands further back where the view is wide (meadow, Rock, peak, open water) and closer in dense wood; a short close shot frames Fuchs with the invited friend before the puzzle opens (not with reduced motion); two fingers pinch the view closer or further within a narrow range (`zoom` in `forest3d.js`, pointer handling in `painted.js`).

Small touches: grass bends round Fuchs and the friends; Fuchs is quicker on paths, wades, slides on snow, hops onto the log bridge and stepping stones and shakes himself dry; the fish's ripple rocks a duck, birds flying up startle the squirrel and the deer; the mailman walks his own round and whistles when a new letter is ready; the haze lifts as Fuchs walks into the meadow, the Rock, the peak or the swamp.

In Frosch's swamp, soft bog ground is blocked even where a hummock rises above the water. Fuchs stays on the wooden Steg: five connected sections start on firm ground at `(-9.5,59)` and lead to his stopping point at `(-27.2,83.7)`, centred on the final deck. Manual movement and planned routes share this boundary. The metre-grid path search connects diagonal crossing cells only along the same deck or crossings sharing an endpoint, then follows their actual ends and joints; ordinary blocked corners still cannot be cut. `tests/swamp-navigation.test.cjs` checks the whole bog, continuous decks and routes to/from every named destination; the browser suite checks real phone/tablet journeys and movement at the deck edge.

The cave (`CAVE` in `layout.js`, `cave` in `terrain.js`, `world/big/cave.js`, `world/big/bats.js`): a passage some thirty-five metres long that winds into the Rock from its south flank and opens into a hall with an underground lake. The terrain is cut flat along the cave's way (`spine`); `cave.js` traces the cut's outline and builds the rock round it: floor, walls and vaulted ceiling in one mesh with stalactites, stalagmites, columns and rubble; the hillside over it and the rock face above the mouth; and black between the two. The inside is not lit by the sun: daylight from the mouth and the glow of crystals, mushrooms and the lake are worked out once per vertex and drawn with the shared rock texture. Its walls face inwards only, so the camera comes down behind Fuchs as he walks in (`caveCamera` in `forest3d.js`) and looks into the cave like into a diorama, even from inside the rock; the sky and the haze go dark with it. The way in is a round arch of mossy rock standing in the hillside (the rock over the mouth is raised to a rounded outcrop, `caveRoofTop`); seen from outside, daylight lies on the threshold and the crystals are only bright stones by day (they glow again at dusk and as Fuchs walks in: `caveLight.inside`). Glow-worms on the ceiling, drops ringing on the lake, drips and echoes (`soundscape.js`); Fuchs leans over the lake. Bats hang from the hall's ceiling: when Fuchs walks in under them they wake, flutter in rounds, a few fly out through the passage and come home later; at dusk some hunt over the mouth; with reduced motion they sleep on. Inside, a tap means a spot on the cave floor; a tap on the hill above means the hill.

Biber's works (`world/big/beaver.js`, built with the `pond` site): the dam is a wall of mud and stones with heavy logs along its foot, a thatch and a mat of bent, tapered sticks (bark reddish, weathered or wet, some peeled pale, gnawed points), poles along both edges of the crest (the middle stays Fuchs' way across), drift caught on the pond side, grass and fresh leafy branches, and water spilling over two low places with foam on the brook below. The lodge is a mound of mud thatched with sticks that point to its top, with a loose crown, an arched way in framed with gnawed logs (a warm light inside at dusk) and a raft of peeled sticks.

The Steinbock (`NEIGHBOURS`, `PLACES` and the `ibex` trail in `layout.js`, `ibex` in `tools/characters/plush.js` and `characters3d.js`) is a letter friend like the others. He stands on his crag at the Rock's rim above the waterfall, looks out over the land and now and then stamps a hoof; his puzzle is **Bergmuster** (30 patterns of stone, fir and flower). The spring runs between the summit trail and his crag: a long flat bridging stone (crossing kind `slab` in `terrain.js`, built with the `falls` site) carries the way over it, and the `ibex` trail leads round the crag to the rim in front of it, where Fuchs stands to meet him. From the cave's path, a few steps before its mouth, the `ibex-cave` trail climbs the Rock's face to the same spot: along the foot of the face, a rounded hairpin, a ledge back across the face, a turn up onto the rim and along it to his crag. `RAMPS` grades it into the cliff (`ramp` in `terrain.js`: the way is level across, rises evenly between the heights given at its points and eases off into level landings at every turn; it is built up below and cut steeply into the rock above, so the rim stays as it was). Its level core counts as walkable whatever the slope beside it (`onRamp`, like the crossings), so the 1 m path grid sees it as one continuous way; `tests/ibex-ramp.test.cjs` checks that the route from the cave's path to the Steinbock takes it. On the drawn map he has his own pin at the crag the painting is calibrated to.

Rätsel instructions play their bundled German recordings, including offline. If a recording cannot play, the app tries a German device voice.

Rock (`rockSteps` and `patch({rock})` in `world/textures.js`, `stone` in `terrain.js`): one wrapping rock texture (bedding lines, cracks, a speckle for glints) is laid from three sides on whatever a mesh marks as bare stone (`rk` per vertex: the Rock's cliffs and stony ground, the cave's roof and mouth, boulders) and read as relief too. Outdoors it shows the rock's cracks and blotches and only a faint hint of the level beds (a whole hill ruled with lines looks unnatural); inside the cave the beds show fully. The ground is a little darker where trees, big rocks, the den tree and the sett stand on it (baked into the terrain's colours).

Stones (`rockGeo` and `weld` in `world/tiles.js`, `lump` in `world/big/wonders.js`): boulders, scree, the stepping stones, the bridging stone and the old stones of the stone circle are welded spheres with shared normals, so they are shaded round instead of in flat facets; the big ones (`boulder`, `bigStone`) use a finer sphere with the same shape, so their outline is smooth too.

Water (`waterMaterial` in `world/big/tiles.js`): the pond, the plunge pool and the swamp look like the brook — the same blue, ripples of about the same size and strength — and take only a little sky, because the camera sees open water from further back and at a flatter angle. Only the streams' flow, streaks and edge foam set running water apart. In the far ring both are drawn in one colour. No morning mist lies over the pond and the plunge pool (the swamp keeps its own).

Where water meets water or land, no edge is drawn by a boundary in the code; a shore is always where the ground comes out of the water (9.3.92). Where the upper brook leaves Fisch's plunge pool and runs into Biber's pond (no dam between), the pool's still water thins out across the pool's mouth while the brook's flowing water comes in (`fade` in `site` and `stream.fade` in `terrain.js`, the `wa.z` attribute in `tiles.js`); the pool's bed colour gives way to the brook's bed in the same way. Still-water corners outside a pool or the bog carry no fade, so the two interpolated factors cannot light up a toothed line between them. The bog lies in a hollow: towards its outer edge a low bank (up to 0.2 m) rises out of its water (`swampQ` and the bank in `rawHill`), so to the west and south, where the land falls away below the water's level, the water ends at a smooth shore instead of at the bog's outline; its shore tint and the change from pond-like shore to bog mud fade gradually. `tests/water-edges.test.cjs` checks these edges.

Distance and loading (`world/streamer.js`, `world/fade.js`, `forest3d.js`): the camera always looks north from behind Fuchs, so the far ring of cheap tiles only covers the wedge it can see, out to where the haze has almost covered the ground (`viewWedge`, about 112 m in the wood). The near ring of full-detail tiles follows the picture too: everything within 22 m of Fuchs and, ahead of him, the ground in the camera's view out to 50 m (a wedge just wider than the picture); none is built behind the camera, and tiles are built in the order the camera sees them, so the detail is ready well before it reaches the top of the picture, also zoomed out. The far tiles' ground takes on the colour of what grows there in the near ring (grass, the meadow's tall grass and its flower drifts). Tiles never pop: a new tile, a tile that changes between its far and near version (old and new in complementary stipples) and a tile that leaves dissolve over 0.6 s. Landmarks are built hidden, their shaders compiled without stalling, and then dissolve in. The haze is light (about 36–112 m in the wood) and the land beyond the tiles is a coarse, sunlit mesh of woods, meadows, rock and snow that fades into the same haze, with the painted ridges behind it.

The sun's shadow map (trees, rocks, landmarks) stands still and is drawn only when needed: its 60 m square keeps the same sharpness, sits round the ground the camera sees and moves by whole texels, so shadows already drawn stay exactly put. It is drawn again when that ground no longer fits, when the sun moves, or soon after trees or a landmark arrive in it or leave it (within their dissolve, at most about every half second). Shadows fade out with the distance from the camera before the square ends (`shadowReach` in `world/textures.js`), so no edge shows. `tests/world-streaming-browser.cjs` walks the real world and checks that nothing pops on screen, there are no holes, the world's edge stays in the haze and trees on screen have their shadows.

Shadows of those who move (`world/shadows.js`): the sun's shadow map stands still, so Fuchs, the mailman and the two friends nearest him each get their own: the character is drawn as a dark shape from where the sun stands into a small picture, and that picture is laid along the sun's rays on a little grid that follows the ground (a hand above it, so it falls on the grass too). Shadows have the character's outline, grow long at dusk and fade in rain and in the cave; the soft blob under the feet stays as a faint contact shade. Not in software rendering (`?software=1`).

Graphics touches: wind sways the plants and leaves; ground detail (`world/big/decals.js`) puts roots, moss, needles, pebbles, twigs and worn patches on the woodland floor; painted ridges (Alps, hills, wooded edge) fill the horizon; characters stand on the lowest ground under their feet, squint happily after tricks and catch a warm rim light at dusk; snails, ladybirds and little frogs live on the ground near Fuchs; the world's buttons are painted wooden medallions (`js/friends/hud-art.js`).
