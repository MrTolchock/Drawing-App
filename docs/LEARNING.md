# Learning scope

Waldpost supports practice for first-grade children in Zürich. It complements teaching; it is not a complete curriculum or a certified assessment. Interface language is Swiss Standard German.

Every active puzzle has an explicit fixed difficulty boundary in the [catalogue](PUZZLE-CATALOGUE.md). A round selects five distinct iterations; retries have no penalty or deadline.

| Area | Current activities |
| --- | --- |
| Quantities and arithmetic | Nüsse zählen, Am Bach zählen, Waldkäfer zählen, Äpfel rechnen, Zahlennachbarn, Glühwürmchen sammeln, Waldpicknick, Beeren vergleichen |
| Patterns, memory and space | Welche Form fehlt?, Waldmuster, Waldschatz, Waldspiegel, Waldpost |
| Nature and sequence | Tierrätsel, Wer wohnt wo?, Naturrätsel, Naturdetektive, Was kommt zuerst? |
| Reading and writing | Reimpaare lesen, Lies und zeig, Wörter und Bilder, Tierwörter lesen, Silben zählen, Waldwörter ergänzen, Silben-Puzzle, Wort und Waldbild, Silbenblätter, Farben lesen, Schreibspuren, Zahlenspuren |

The reviewed removals of Nüsse verdoppeln, Waldwerkstatt and Waldumfrage leave 31 active puzzles. Drawing and colouring screens are retired; historical artwork and saves remain intact.

Ordinary game rules should be understood through pictures, reversible manipulation, an optional spoken rule explanation. Reading tasks intentionally require reading. Waldwörter ergänzen practises written letters in pictured words; the picture alone does not remove that reading requirement. Speech depends on installed German device voices, including offline availability. No recorded narration is bundled.

[The puzzle blueprint](../design/PUZZLE-BLUEPRINT.md) describes current interactions and review requirements. [Research records](RESEARCH.md) preserve the original source work; their old feature inventories do not define the current app.

Physical device checks and observation with children remain necessary to evaluate comprehension, accessibility and learning suitability. Automated content and browser checks cover correctness and interaction behaviour, not pedagogical efficacy.
