# Design revision 9 — 7 November 2026

## Recorded user feedback

The old Reimzirkus is a useful reading-led model. Blaumeise, owl and beaver were unclear. Several animals should share simple categories, such as Reading and Mathematics. After the revision the user endorsed the examples and simpler categories, found Waldkauz a little difficult, and requested the blueprint update and a complete GitHub push.

## Current decision

- Main categories: Mathematics, Reading, Writing, Nature, Creativity. The initial five activities cover the first two; the remaining categories organise later work.
- Reading hosts: Maus, Blaumeise, Waldkauz. Mathematics hosts: Eichhörnchen, Biber. A category can contain several puzzle types, introduced by different animals. Supporting skills are curriculum tags, not animal-specific categories.
- Blaumeise: old-game written word/picture rhyme cards; two choices; optional separate word audio.
- Waldkauz: easier three-word action sentences with two pictures: Die Maus sitzt. / Der Vogel fliegt. / Die Maus rennt. / Der Vogel sitzt. / Der Fisch schwimmt. The target stays visible and is not narrated automatically. A separate sleeping-mouse example explains the match. No initial spatial-preposition contrasts or story sequencing.
- Biber: choose the missing shape in a pictured house/cart. One tap fills it; no coordinate grid. This is a basic geometry warm-up.
- Five tasks, one difficulty level, retries, automatic progression and one-time pictured host-resource rewards remain. Shared category does not mean identical first-visit activities.

## Preserved references and implementation boundary

Original art, frozen v1/v2, screenshots, integrity manifest and v8 historical specimens are unchanged. review.html and review-v9.html use v9 overrides with the preserved v8 base. No shipped Heart Tree runtime/save files change. The current review resets on refresh; production saving remains required.

Blueprint, curriculum matrix, puzzle specifications, experience design and cloud handoff follow these decisions. Expand to 15 reviewed tasks per puzzle and exclude demonstration pairs/objects from scored pools before release. User endorsement does not constitute teacher review, child testing, a scope freeze or mastery evidence. Basic geometry may be easy for some 7–8-year-olds.
