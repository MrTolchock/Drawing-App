# A forest with room for many puzzles

> Archived design/review note. Implementation and approval statements describe the stage when it was written. See the [current app notes](../../docs/HEART-TREE.md) and [design index](../README.md) for the present build.

Design extension for Farbtupf — Der Herzbaum: Die vergessenen Pfade. Planning only; the application is unchanged.

The five clearings, Swiss-native cast, opening bridge quest and Meet → Reconnect → Gather story remain the foundation. Ten story milestones organise a growing collection of short quests, puzzle activities, practice and creative work.

Every beginner entry needs to be understandable before reading. Guidance follows [See it, hear it, try it](BEFORE-READING.md): a visible goal, short spoken action, demonstration, fresh attempt and repeatable help. Reading ability and learning difficulty are separate.

## 1. What grows

Keep these units distinct:

| Unit | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story milestone | An important relationship or world change | Reopen the garden path |
| Short quest episode | A small request with a beginning, activity and result | Help prepare a trail marker |
| Puzzle activity | A set of interaction and learning rules | A memory game, quantity comparison or reflected grid |
| Content pack or variant | New material used by an activity | A reviewed set of native woodland objects or words |
| Round | One play session in an activity at a chosen level | One existing round of questions |
| Question | One task inside a round | One quantity, word or observation prompt |
| Activity place | A persistent location that introduces quests or practice | Beaver’s worktable |

A milestone can contain several short quest episodes. One activity can support several episodes through suitable contexts and content. Each activity can also be played directly for practice.

New content packs can reuse existing activity rules. New puzzle mechanics receive a new flat activity and the same world-to-puzzle transition and completion contract. Both routes can join the existing forest.

The first bridge introduction stays short: one appropriate existing round and its immediate payoff. Larger projects later can unfold over several small sessions.

## 2. Geography with room to grow

Use three modest activity places in each clearing as the initial spatial design. They are recognisable things in the environment, rather than a field of quest markers. Content can keep growing at these places.

| Clearing | Resident meeting point | A place for recurring activities | A place for discovery or creativity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beaver’s brook | Beaver at the crossing | A small natural-wood worktable | The water’s edge and observation stone |
| Hare’s meadow garden | Hare by the garden path | A small garden sorting surface | The welcoming corner and leaf-sign place |
| Hedgehog’s moss grove | Hedgehog near the resting spot | An old trail marker and mossy stones | A sheltered nook for memory, observation and drawing |
| Owl’s story hollow | Owl at the hollow | A picture-book stand | A low sign-writing and drawing surface |
| Kingfisher’s reed pond | Kingfisher at the perch | A small observation notebook | The pond-viewing spot and reeds |

These places host a mix of skills. Owl can ask a number question about story characters; Beaver can need a word or picture sign; Hare can offer patterns, sharing, nature or creative work. Subject placement follows a meaningful request and appropriate level.

Three activity places per clearing are a planning default. Existing places can host additional collections before any extra geography is needed. Reserve discreet routes for possible future side clearings in the world plan, without showing unavailable-content locks or empty “coming soon” attractions to the child.

### An illustrative capacity

**5 clearings × 3 activity places × 8 short episodes = 120 quest episodes.**

This illustrates how much content the chosen geography can hold. The episodes have not been authored or implemented. A quest count, activity count, round count and question count describe different things.

The activity catalogue has room to register further puzzle families; it is not tied to the number of physical places. A hundred activities can share the same set of world entry points, provided each has a suitable flat screen, content and launch contract.

## 3. Four ways to encounter activities

| Route | Player experience | How it affects the world |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main story projects | Short quests contribute to a shared restoration or gathering milestone | Authored project stages change a path, shared place or the tree |
| Optional local stories | A resident has another small request, visit or observation to share | A finite scene detail, story memory or relationship response can appear |
| Practice | Choose a familiar activity and appropriate level | Existing learning progress and rewards are recorded; the repaired world stays intact |
| Creative encounters | Use existing painting, drawing or saved pictures for a personal contribution | A selected drawing can appear on a sign, book page or display |

Every route uses the same familiar flat activity interface where relevant. Story and practice launches carry explicit context so a practice round cannot accidentally trigger the wrong world event.

Optional content stays available after chapter changes and after the tree awakens. A restored place can keep offering new stories, observations and practice.

## 4. A larger project, played in small pieces

Example: **The Garden Path**, one of the ten story milestones.

| Small episode | Narrative purpose | Possible activity family, subject to later review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sign for the path | Hare wants the next neighbour to find the garden | Pictures, words, patterns or tracing |
| Hedgehog remembers the route | Compare the old route’s landmarks | Memory, sequencing or routes |
| Open the shared connection | Bring the neighbours’ contributions together | A suitable final short activity if the authored story needs one |
| More to notice | Explore the new route after the milestone | Optional nature, comparison, drawing and practice episodes |

The exact required contributions are authored for that project. Optional rounds do not turn into an accumulating completion quota.

Each small episode has its own stopping point and saved result. The child can finish one and leave the larger project for another visit.

Once the project is complete, the path stays open. Further episodes at that place use the restored setting. New activities do not require rebuilding the same bridge or clearing the same path repeatedly.

## 5. A large library with a calm interface

The first session still presents one main goal and two nearby clearings.

After the opening:

- Keep one current small goal visible.
- Offer a small local set, usually two or three invitations: a story step, an optional local episode and a way to practice.
- Use an animal portrait, short spoken line and one picture to explain each invitation.
- Use an activity preview to show what happens; choosing a game must not depend on reading its title.
- Start unfamiliar interactions with a short demonstration and a fresh supported attempt. Keep replay available when rules are familiar.
- Reveal more invitations when the child finishes one, visits another activity place or opens a collection.
- Keep the complete catalogue accessible through the practice book at the Heart Tree.
- Support simple picture-led browsing by familiar place, activity kind and chosen learning stage.
- Keep invitations available between visits; additional episodes need no daily deadline or waiting period.

The game should remember the child’s choice of active quest while allowing wandering and practice. Changing the active invitation does not discard finished steps.

A growing catalogue must not fill the 3D map with hundreds of markers. Activity places stay readable; catalogue and collection browsing provide depth when requested.

## 7. Future content organisation

This describes a future design contract, not implemented code.

| Record | What it needs to describe |
| --- | --- |
| Puzzle activity | Stable identity, flat screen, stages, input and completion behaviour, variants, subject tags, assets, spoken instruction, demonstration, guided example, reading target and audio policy |
| Quest episode | Stable identity, resident, activity place, short request and response, chosen activity or suitable activity options, availability and required story contributions |
| Collection | A readable set of related episodes, local art and an explicit place in the forest |
| Story milestone | Explicit required contribution identities, optional episodes, one-time world change and chapter effect |
| World state | Which paths, shared places and tree stages are restored |
| Learning progress | Existing activity-stage completion, stars, stickers and saved work |
| Launch context | Active quest or direct practice, chosen level, world position, camera and return checkpoint |

A new episode can refer to an existing activity without adding another screen or 3D building. A new content pack can offer suitable variation while using the same activity. A new mechanic plugs into the flat activity contract.

Required assets belong to their activity or collection. The 3D scene keeps a bounded number of models and interaction places while the catalogue grows. Only the selected flat activity needs to be active at a time. Offline asset caching and pack size will be validated during implementation.

Instruction and demonstration assets can be shared by activity family and interaction version. New content using a familiar rule need not introduce another full tutorial. A genuinely different rule needs its own explanation and comprehension review.

The [first-session walkthrough](FIRST-SESSION.md) supplies concrete quantity, pattern and missing-object examples, draft spoken lines and a blank observation sheet. Its reference question content remains distinct from the later full puzzle review.

## 8. Progress that survives a growing library

- Keep activity identity, quest identity, story milestones and world state separate.
- Preserve existing learning-storage identifiers and reward rules.
- Use stable content identities independent of display names and catalogue order.
- Each authored milestone refers to explicit required contributions; it must not require “all quests currently in this collection”.
- Adding optional episodes or a new puzzle family must not undo or lengthen a completed milestone.
- A previously earned learning star does not block a fresh story episode using the same activity.
- An already-applied world payoff is not applied again during practice.
- In-progress projects retain their completed contributions when content is added.
- The awake tree and earlier repairs remain after new post-story collections arrive.
- Keep difficulty and story chapter independent.
- Keep reading ability separate from activity difficulty; the main story needs a complete pre-reader route.
- Distinguish instruction audio, reading targets and activities whose learning goal requires hearing sound.
- Save a safe return checkpoint for the active quest; assess detailed interrupted-round persistence per activity.

Learning practice can therefore continue for much longer than the first story, with new activities joining the forest without resetting the child’s accomplishments.

## 9. Visual feedback at scale

The world has a finite set of meaningful restoration states: quiet, being helped, restored and used by neighbours. A major project changes those states.

Optional stories can be remembered through a small scene detail, dialogue, a book page or a chosen drawing. Practice gives the existing activity feedback and appropriate learning progress.

This keeps the forest readable as the number of completed rounds grows. A hundred completed rounds do not require a hundred extra bridges, benches or glowing markers.

## 10. Swiss-native content rule

Use the native cast and plant palette in the main blueprint for new world and quest art. Record the species used when a content pack introduces plants or animals, and check that its habitat and season are plausible.

Existing exercise content still needs its separate review. Listing an existing activity in this inventory does not certify that all of its pictures already meet the new ecology rule.

Creative prompts can encourage observation of the approved woodland species. Existing educational colour meanings, handwriting geometry and difficulty options remain intact.

## 11. Build plan refinement

When implementation is authorised:

1. Build the selected opening with one appropriate flat activity and the reviewed picture, voice and demonstration guidance.
2. Add a second activity and several additional episodes at the same brook or garden place, proving that content can grow without changing the map.
3. Add the practice book and small invitation selection.
4. Expand the five clearings and authored story projects.
5. Integrate reviewed existing activities and collections incrementally.
6. Validate content updates, offline use, saved progress, catalogue navigation and target-device performance.

The initial implementation should establish the catalogue and quest contracts before the full content library is attached.

Validation needs to include:

- Twenty additional episode records can be attached to existing places without adding 3D scenery or overwhelming the visible invitations.
- A different flat activity can use the same launch, completion and world-return contract.
- Practice completion, story completion and existing star rewards remain distinct.
- The current chapter and restored world survive adding a new optional collection.
- A young child can find a suitable next activity without reading a long list.
- A child who cannot read can understand the activity’s goal, action, help and feedback through the planned guidance.
- Comprehension is observed with children rather than inferred from illustrations or a title.
- All reviewed difficulty stages remain selectable.
- Native-species and content-review status are visible to the authoring workflow, not presented as technical information in the child’s game.
