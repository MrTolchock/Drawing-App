# Selected visual direction — original A, Painted woodland

**Status: selected by the user.** The original balance of detail and open space
remains the visual reference for the forest and its flat puzzle pages.

## Authoritative reference

Use the **left column, A Painted woodland**, in the intact [original A/B board](../archive/woodland-studies/visual-middle-ground-options.png).
Its upper image defines the calmer map treatment; its lower image defines the

The mixed comparison board is stored in the archive because it also contains

## Qualities to retain

- Warm ivory light, soft yellow-sage and forest greens, dusty blue water and a
  clear orange fox accent.
- Broad painted canopies, natural trunks and roots, restrained brush texture
  and rounded stones.
- Quiet ground between landmarks, continuous sandy paths and sparse low
  vegetation. Keep original A's balance of detail and open space.
- Group detail near residents, banks and tree bases. Characters, paths and
  meaningful objects stay easy to distinguish.
- An expressive quadrupedal orange fox, green neckerchief, cream muzzle and
  white tail tip; warm, readable resident silhouettes.
- Shared artwork between exploration, guidance, pictured returns, the book and
  flat puzzles. Additional collections belong in familiar places and the book.

## World and interaction

The angled overhead Three.js world supplies movement, depth, safe routes,
bridges and persistent repairs. Its current illustrated presentation places
painted characters and vegetation in that scene. The puzzles remain flat.
The fox stays visible, obstructing foreground trees fade, and transparent
corners do not intercept path taps. The [app notes](../../docs/HEART-TREE.md)
describe the implementation and checks.

Animation supports walking, resident gestures, repairs and the tree response.
Quiet areas stay calm when nothing needs attention. Picture controls, short
speech and separate examples support children who cannot yet read.

## Swiss-native palette

The Heart Tree is a pedunculate oak. Supporting woodland plants are beech, hazel,
alder, moss, wild strawberry and common reeds. Residents are Eurasian beaver,
European brown hare, European hedgehog, tawny owl and common kingfisher. Retain
the hare's long dark-tipped ears, the beaver's broad tail and the owl's round
head without ear tufts.

The selected direction is recorded separately from implementation validation.
Physical iPad/Safari comfort, audible guidance and a non-reading child's
understanding still need the observation described in the current app notes.
