# Two woodland directions between Soft woodland and the original

> Archived design/review note. Implementation and approval statements describe the stage when it was written. See the [current app notes](../../docs/HEART-TREE.md) and [design index](../README.md) for the present build.

Selection complete: the user chose **the original A, Painted woodland**, in the left column of this board. See the [chosen art direction](ART-DIRECTION.md). Other options remain archived; application implementation remains deferred.

This round compared **two intermediate options** between the earlier **2. Soft woodland** and the original detailed forest, restoring some natural richness while keeping clear paths and quieter spaces. **A is selected.**

[View both options — map above, future 3D world below](visual-middle-ground-options.png).

| Option | Position between the references | Visual treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Painted woodland** | Closer to Soft woodland | Broad rounded forms and plenty of open ground, with restrained brush texture, more natural canopy edges, a few roots and grouped bank stones. A small amount of detail makes the woodland feel alive. |
| **B. Storybook woodland** | Closer to the original | Richer canopy shapes, branching trunks, warmer greens and more character around resident landmarks. Details gather in small groups, with quieter ground around paths and characters. |

The difference is the amount and placement of natural detail within the same visual family. Both use sculpted forms with painted surfaces; neither introduces a new paper-cut or photorealistic style.

## What makes the middle ground calm

- Keep the route, fox and resident landmarks clear. Extra leaves, moss and stones belong in a few groups near scene edges or places of interest.
- Let the map simplify more than the close 3D view. The overview communicates where places are; the walkable scene can show their character.
- Use broad canopy masses and ground colours before small details. Leave open space around interactive objects.
- Keep warm ivory light, forest greens, blue water and the orange fox. Avoid a scene-wide layer of luminous trails, flowers and decorative movement.
- Retain Swiss-native woodland species, the brown hare and the round-headed tawny owl, with recognisable silhouettes at reduced detail.
- Keep the large puzzle collection within the established clearings and picture-led book. More puzzles do not require more visible map objects.

The Heart Tree story, five-clearing loop, bridge opening, non-reader guidance and flat puzzle artwork continue as planned. The comparison contains generated visual concepts; it does not implement a map, movement or a Three.js scene. Application code remains unchanged.
